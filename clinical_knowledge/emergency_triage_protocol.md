# Emergency Department Triage and Acuity Scale Protocol

## 1. Overview and Purpose
This standard operating procedure defines the 5-level Emergency Severity Index (ESI) protocol used across all HIMES hospital triage stations. Every incoming emergency patient must be assessed within 5 minutes of arrival.

## 2. ESI Triage Levels
- **ESI Level 1 (Resuscitation)**: Immediate life-threatening condition requiring immediate aggressive intervention (e.g., cardiac arrest, respiratory arrest, severe trauma with shock, SpO2 < 80%, unresponsive patient). Doctor response time: 0 minutes (Immediate).
- **ESI Level 2 (Emergent)**: High-risk situation, confused/lethargic/disoriented, or severe pain/distress (e.g., active chest pain suggestive of ACS, stroke symptoms within 4.5 hours window, severe asthma exacerbation, ectopic pregnancy suspicion). Doctor response time: < 10 minutes.
- **ESI Level 3 (Urgent)**: Stable vitals but requires 2 or more diagnostic/therapeutic resources (e.g., abdominal pain requiring labs and CT, moderate fracture requiring x-ray and reduction). Doctor response time: < 30 minutes.
- **ESI Level 4 (Less Urgent)**: Requires 1 resource (e.g., simple laceration requiring sutures, uncomplicated UTI needing urinalysis and antibiotic prescription). Doctor response time: < 60 minutes.
- **ESI Level 5 (Non-urgent)**: Requires 0 resources (e.g., medication refill, stitch removal, mild rash). Doctor response time: < 120 minutes.

## 3. Pediatric Vital Signs Flagging Criteria
Immediate physician escalation is required for pediatric vitals exceeding:
- Age < 3 months: Heart rate > 180 or < 100 bpm; Resp Rate > 60 bpm.
- Age 3-12 months: Heart rate > 160 or < 90 bpm; Resp Rate > 50 bpm.
- Age 1-3 years: Heart rate > 150 bpm; Resp Rate > 40 bpm; Temperature > 39.0°C with lethargy.
