# Intensive Care Unit (ICU) Admission and Discharge Policy

## 1. Admission Criteria
Patients are prioritized into four categories:
- **Priority 1 (Critically ill, unstable)**: Patients requiring intensive monitoring and treatment that cannot be provided outside an ICU (e.g., invasive mechanical ventilation, continuous vasoactive infusions, post-cardiac arrest, septic shock with multi-organ dysfunction).
- **Priority 2 (Intensive monitoring required)**: Patients who are at risk of immediate decompensation requiring invasive monitoring lines (e.g., severe acute pancreatitis, post-neurosurgical monitoring, unstable diabetic ketoacidosis).
- **Priority 3 (Unstable, limited prognosis)**: Patients with severe acute illness superimposed on underlying terminal illness; intubation or resuscitation may be limited by advance directives.
- **Priority 4 (Not appropriate for ICU)**: Too well to benefit from ICU (managed in step-down/ward) OR terminal, irreversible illness where death is imminent and ICU provides no curative benefit.

## 2. Discharge / Transfer Criteria
A patient is considered ready for transfer from ICU to Step-Down or General Ward when:
1. Hemodynamically stable without vasopressors or inotropes for >= 12 hours.
2. Successfully extubated and maintaining SpO2 >= 94% on room air or low-flow nasal cannula (<= 4L/min) for >= 24 hours.
3. Stable cardiac rhythm without life-threatening arrhythmias for >= 24 hours.
4. Conscious, oriented, and able to protect their own airway.
5. Arterial line and central venous catheter removed or deemed safe for ward management.
